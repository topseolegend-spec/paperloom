How to Make a Monthly Budget

Most budgets fail in the same two ways: savings is whatever is left at the end, and the irregular costs are missing entirely. Fix those two and a budget mostly works on its own.

Write the income first, and be honest about it

Take-home pay, not the headline figure. If your income varies - freelance work, commission, a shop - use the lowest of the last three months rather than the average. A budget built on a good month is a budget that fails in a normal one.

Savings goes above "everything else", not below it

This is the single change that makes the difference. Saving what is left at the end of the month means saving what you failed to spend, which is almost nothing. Move a fixed amount on the day the money arrives and budget the rest - the spending adjusts, quietly and without much pain.

List fixed costs, then variable ones

  1. Fixed: rent, utilities, school fees, loan payments, insurance. These are the same every month and you cannot negotiate them this week.
  2. Savings: the amount you decided above.
  3. Variable: groceries, transport, eating out, everything else. This is the part that flexes - and groceries is the line a week of planned meals moves most.

The costs that come twice a year

Car servicing, a school uniform, Eid, a wedding, an insurance renewal. These are what break a budget that balanced perfectly on paper. Marking them on a year calendar first is the quickest way to find the ones you have forgotten. Add up a year of them, divide by twelve, and put that figure in as a monthly line - even if the money just sits there most months. That line is the reason the budget survives March.

Track for two months before you judge it

The first month tells you what you actually spend, which is rarely what you assumed. The second tells you whether the first was typical. Changing the numbers before you have two months of data is guessing with extra steps.

Why paper

An app that categorises everything automatically removes the only part that changes behaviour: noticing. Writing "42,000 - groceries" takes four seconds, and those four seconds are where the thinking happens. The budget sheets here are built for that - one line per category, filled in by hand.
