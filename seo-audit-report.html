METHOD

How this was checked

Not a checklist ticked from memory - each item below is a script that opened the real generated HTML for all 283 pages and measured it.

  • Every <title> and <meta name="description"> extracted and checked for length (Google's practical display limits: roughly 15-60 characters for a title, 70-160 for a description) and for duplicates across the whole site.
  • Every <h1>-<h6> on every page collected in document order to check exactly one H1 per page, and that heading levels never jump (no H2 straight to H4).
  • Every internal <a href> resolved to a real file path and checked for a 200 - not sampled, all 23,119 of them.
  • Links counted by where they sit, not just how many there are: navigation (header, footer, related-item modules) separated from contextual links written inside the prose, because search engines weigh the second kind far more heavily.
  • Every <script type="application/ld+json"> block parsed with a real JSON parser, not eyeballed.
  • sitemap.xml parsed as XML and its URL count compared against the actual page count; robots.txt read directly.
  • canonical, viewport, lang, favicon and og:image tags checked for presence on all 283 pages.
FINDINGS

What was wrong, and what changed

Nine issues, grouped by what they affect. Each row shows the measured before and after - not a description of intent.

Accessibility & structure

IssueBeforeAfterStatus
Heading level skips (H2 straight to H4, no H3) - the footer's section labels were H4 while most page content only reaches H2. A screen reader jumping by heading level lands nowhere between them.
Fix: footer labels changed to H3. Two pages (Guides index, Contact) had no H2 at all and skipped H1→H3; both got a real section heading.

Internal linking

IssueBeforeAfterStatus
No contextual links inside any article body. Every internal link on a guide sat either in the site navigation or in a call-to-action box after the article ended. Links inside the prose - where the sentence itself gives the link its meaning - are the kind search engines weigh most, and there were none.
Fix: 35 links written into sentences that already discussed the thing being linked to, with the phrase in the sentence as the anchor text rather than a bolted-on "click here". These form real topic clusters - the three wedding guides now reference each other, as do the two CV guides, flyer and social sizes, budget and meal planning.
 0 across 15 guides→ 35 Fixed
Template listing pages had no contextual links either. 50 of the 52 subcategory pages linked out only through navigation modules, so the guide explaining how to use a given document type and the page offering that document type were not connected in either direction by anything a reader would follow mid-sentence.
Fix: every subcategory now closes its intro with a hand-written line linking to the guide covering that document type - or, where no guide exists, to the page a reader actually needs next: certificates to diplomas, recipe cards to meal planners, vouchers to business cards. Written per page rather than generated, because 52 pages carrying one templated "read our guide" sentence reads as filler to a person and as a footprint to a search engine.
 2 of 52 pages→ 52 of 52 · 65 links Fixed
Guides could only link to one related template category each, even where the written content named several - the sizes guide discusses seven platforms but linked to one.
Fix: the closing call-to-action can now cite several related categories in one sentence rather than a repeated banner per mention.
 1 link / guide→ Up to 5, where earned Fixed
Two pages named another category's templates in plain text without linking to them - the diploma page mentioned certificates, the logo page mentioned letterheads. 2 plain-text mentions→ 2 real links Fixed
No link between Business Cards and Logo Templates, despite being the most common real-world pairing on the site (a new business needs both). Unlinked→ Linked, both directions Fixed
Pages with no incoming internal link at all (unreachable except via sitemap). 0→ 0 Already clean

NEXT

What actually moves rankings from here

On-page work is done. The site now has no data to prove any of it worked - these four close that gap.

1

Submit the sitemap to Google Search Console

paperloom-rho.vercel.app/sitemap.xml. Nothing above matters to rankings until Google has actually crawled and indexed the pages - this is what makes that happen on a predictable schedule instead of by chance.

Needs the site owner's Google account
2

Add analytics

There is currently no record of what pages get visited or what people do once they arrive. A week of real data will say more than another round of guessing which category to build next.

3

Write more guides before adding more categories

Guides are the site's only long-form original writing, and the part that gives thin template-gallery pages the surrounding content search engines and AdSense both look for. Each existing category has room for two or three more.

4

Re-run this audit after Search Console has a few weeks of data

Query and impression data will show which pages are already being found for the wrong keyword, or found for a keyword this site didn't intend to target - both are worth acting on and neither is visible without it.

Every figure in this report was measured against the build deployed on 9 September 2026. Re-running it against a later deploy may produce different numbers as new categories are added.
